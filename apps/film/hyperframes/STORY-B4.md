# Film B4: "Can I return these shoes?"

The fourth cut of film B. Keeps B3's intro and outro (the owner likes them) and B3's two kinds of
design-system morph. Everything between them is rewritten as a story someone wants to watch, then
directed like a motion-designed product film: the camera moves, the UI is zoomed, pushed, tilted and
annotated, and hand-drawn doodles point at what matters. About 64 seconds.

## The story in one breath

Maya wants to return a pair of shoes. She asks the shop's assistant. It answers with a wall of
instructions, the kind everyone has scrolled through. Then the same question gets a different answer:
the screen she actually needs, already filled in. She taps once and she's done. And it isn't one shop's
trick: the same thing works in her bank, her council, any app, each in its own design, each checked
before she sees it. That's Polyxd.

Every scene exists to move that story on. Nothing is shown because it's a feature.

## The look of the motion

- **Doodles** in the brand: a marker line in ink (#141413) and signal orange (#FF6E40), drawn on (stroke
  reveal, 250–500 ms), slightly wobbly like a real pen, handwritten notes in a hand font (Caveat or
  Patrick Hand). Circles around things, arrows that point, underlines, a scribbled strike-through,
  little ticks, a "7 steps?!" counter, sparkles on the reveal. They arrive, make their point, and are
  erased or blown away before the next thing happens. Never more than two on screen.
- **Camera**: the whole stage is a camera. Push in on the thing that matters, pull out to reveal
  context, a slow drift while holding, a whip pan between apps, a gentle 3D tilt of the phone on the
  reveal. Motion follows the music's beats. Use the hyperframes-keyframes skill for punch-ins, Ken
  Burns drifts, whip pans, masks and FLIP moves; hyperframes-animation blueprints for scene structure
  and transitions; hyperframes-registry for any named effect before hand-building one.
- **One focus at a time**: a doodle, a caption or a camera move leads each moment; they don't compete.
- **Type**: captions in Young Serif, set large and animated in (word-by-word or line mask reveals),
  never parked in a corner. At most one line on screen.

## Rules that still hold

- One idea at a time; a transition ends before the next begins; captions hold long enough to read twice.
- The UI is the real renderer, never a mockup.
- Doodles and captions never cover the thing they point at.
- Music under everything from the first frame to the last chord; sound effects under the music.
